Discrete-Time Markov Chains in Longitudinal Market Dynamics

Patient switching across therapeutic classes is modeled as a discrete stochastic process. Let state space $S = \{\text{Brand Alpha}, \text{Comp Beta}, \text{Comp Gamma}, \text{Discontinued}\}$. The transition probability matrix $\mathbf{P}$ satisfies $\sum_j P_{ij} = 1$:

$$P_{ij} = \mathbb{P}\left(X_{t+1} = j \;\middle|\; X_t = i\right)$$

Given an initial market share vector $\mathbf{s}_0$, the projected market share distribution after $k$ treatment cycles is determined by the Chapman-Kolmogorov forward equation:

$$\mathbf{s}_k = \mathbf{s}_0 \mathbf{P}^k$$

Net Switching Balance: $\text{Net Switch} = \sum_{j \neq \text{Brand}} (\text{Switch-In}_{j \to \text{Brand}} - \text{Switch-Out}_{\text{Brand} \to j})$. A positive net switch drives organic market share growth even when total naive starts plateau.
