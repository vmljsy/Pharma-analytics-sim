Compliance & Adherence: PDC vs MPR

Why CMS and Medicare Star Ratings standardized on Proportion of Days Covered (PDC) over Medication Possession Ratio (MPR) to eliminate early-refill distortion

PDC vs MPR: The Mathematical Distinction

Measuring real-world medication adherence is critical for clinical outcomes and CMS star ratings. Two primary metrics exist in health informatics:

Medication Possession Ratio (MPR)

$$\text{MPR} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k \text{Days of Supply}_i}{\text{Observation Period (Days)}} \times 100\%$$

The Overlap Flaw: Sums raw days of supply without shifting overlaps. If a patient stockpiles or refills early, MPR can exceed $100\%$ or falsely score $100\%$ despite having a large 2-month gap at year-end!

Proportion of Days Covered (PDC)

$$\text{PDC} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{I}(\text{Day } t \text{ is covered})}{\text{Observation Period (Days)}} \times 100\%$$

The PQA Gold Standard: Overlapping supply days are pushed forward into future non-covered days. PDC is mathematically bounded in $[0\%, 100\%]$. Patients with $\text{PDC} \ge 80\%$ are classified as clinically adherent.
