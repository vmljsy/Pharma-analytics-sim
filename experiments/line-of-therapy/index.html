Oncology Regimen Identification & LoT Advancement Rules

In oncology and autoimmune diseases, patients frequently receive multi-drug regimens rather than single-agent monotherapy. Health economists and real-world evidence (RWE) scientists partition longitudinal claims into Lines of Therapy (LoT) using standardized clinical heuristics:

  • Regimen Definition Window ($W_{\text{regimen}}$): Multi-agent regimens (e.g. Carboplatin + Pemetrexed + Pembrolizumab) are rarely dispensed on the exact same second. A window (typically 28 days) from the first antineoplastic claim bundles all initiated agents into the index regimen.
  • Agent Switch: Replacement of a core agent by an agent of another drug class after $W_{\text{regimen}}$ signifies disease progression or toxicity, triggering an advance to the next line (e.g. $1\text{L} \to 2\text{L}$).
  • Drop-to-Maintenance: Dropping chemotherapy after 4–6 cycles while continuing targeted/immuno maintenance does not trigger a new line under standard NCCN/ASCO heuristics.
  • Treatment Holiday: A therapy-free interval $> 90$ or $120$ days without claims marks the end of active line therapy.
