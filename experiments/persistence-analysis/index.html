Treatment Persistence & Kaplan-Meier Survival

Model time-to-discontinuation (TTD), Greenwood confidence intervals, right-censoring, and the permissible gap sensitivity paradox in real-world longitudinal cohorts

Kaplan-Meier Survival Estimation in Real-World Evidence (RWE)

Treatment persistence measures the duration of continuous medication adherence from index initiation to discontinuation. Because study periods end and patients may disenroll (loss to follow-up), persistence data is inherently right-censored.

The non-parametric Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimator accounts for censoring at discrete event times $t_i$:

$$\hat{S}(t) = \prod_{t_i \le t} \left( 1 - \frac{d_i}{n_i} \right)$$

Where $d_i$ is the number of discontinuation events at time $t_i$, and $n_i$ is the number of patients at risk immediately prior to $t_i$. Standard error is calculated using Greenwood's formula:

$$\widehat{\text{SE}}(\hat{S}(t)) = \hat{S}(t) \sqrt{ \sum_{t_i \le t} \frac{d_i}{n_i (n_i - d_i)} }$$

The Permissible Gap Sensitivity Paradox: A patient who missed medication for 45 days is classified as "Discontinued" under a strict 30-day allowable gap rule, but as "Persistent" under a lenient 60-day rule. Tuning gap tolerance dramatically shifts median persistence!
