The Open-Network Surveillance Dilemma

In closed-payer data (e.g., Medicare FFS, MarketScan Commercial), insurance enrollment tables explicitly identify when a patient leaves the plan. In syndicated open-switch claims (IQVIA LRX, Symphony), there are no enrollment rosters:

1. Naive Open-Network Blindness

$$\text{Rx Absence} \ne \text{Clinical Discontinuation}$$

When a patient switches to a non-reporting specialty pharmacy, mail-order provider, or hospital 340B dispensary, claims abruptly vanish. Naive models classify this as drug abandonment, creating severe downward bias in Kaplan-Meier curves.

2. Pharmacy Activity Floor Proxy

$$\mathbb{I}(\text{Active at } t) = \mathbb{I}\left(\exists \, \text{Claim of any class in } [t - W_{\text{pre}}, t + W_{\text{post}}]\right)$$

Activity Filtering: By requiring ongoing baseline prescription activity in unrelated chronic maintenance classes (e.g., statins, antihypertensives), we censor lost patients rather than falsely marking them as therapy failures.
